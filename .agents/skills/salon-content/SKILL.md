# Skill: Salon Content

> Use this every time content is written for a Sahni Bridal Studio page, blog, caption, or marketing copy.

## Purpose
Produce copy that reads warm-premium-trustworthy, converts bridal search intent, and stays on-brand without sounding salesy or generic.

## Voice adjectives (5, ranked)
1. **Warm** — trusted aunt, not a corporate brand
2. **Premium** — quiet confidence, no fake luxury language
3. **Trustworthy** — facts first, claims always backed
4. **Rooted** — Lahar, 1994, local, not a chain
5. **Feminine** — graceful without being flowery

## Voice anti-patterns (never do)
- Salesy urgency ("Don't miss out!", "Last chance!")
- Corporate hedge language ("world-class", "premier", "unparalleled")
- Overused bridal cliches ("dream day", "fairytale", "once in a lifetime")
- Self-congratulation without a fact ("we are the best" without a review count)
- Emoji clusters in body copy (single emoji in social only, never on site)
- Exclamation stacking

## Bilingual rules
Default is English. Hindi phrases are used **deliberately**, not constantly.

**Use Hindi when:**
- Direct emotional moments on About/Bridal (sparingly, once)
- Greeting in WhatsApp templates
- CTA microcopy when emotionally warmer than English ("Baat kariye" alongside "Chat with us")
- Town page intros that acknowledge local context

**Do not use Hindi when:**
- It would break SEO (primary keywords stay in English)
- It would feel forced or performative
- In form labels (accessibility first)

**Hinglish is fine** in Instagram captions and WhatsApp. Not on the website body.

## Address the reader
- "You" (the bride) — not "our clients"
- "We" (the studio) — after first full mention of Sahni Bridal Studio
- Ankit and Kusum can be named on About + Team, not on service pages

## Bridal-specific language
- "Bride" not "client" on Bridal page
- "Look" not "style" for bridal transformations
- "Package" not "deal"
- "Consultation" not "appointment" for pre-bridal
- "Trial" for test makeup
- "Rental" not "for hire"

## Pricing presentation
- Always include ₹ symbol, no decimals unless the price has paise
- Packages: show inclusions as bullets, price at the top
- "Starts at ₹X" is allowed when multiple tiers exist
- Never hide price behind "contact us"
- If price varies: `Bridal packages from ₹25,000 based on look complexity and event duration.`
- Off-site services: `+ travel charges from Lahar` — disclose upfront

## Before/after caption format
`[Bride or bride name] — [look name] for [event type].
Makeup: [artist name or Sahni Bridal Studio]. Lehenga: [Sahni rental or client's own].`

- 2 lines max
- Name only if bride consented (default: "Bride")
- Rental credit when it's our rental (differentiator)

## CTA placement rules
- Primary CTA above the fold on every page
- Secondary CTA at end of every major section, not every paragraph
- Sticky WhatsApp button is always present — do not duplicate inside copy
- CTA verbs: Book, Chat, Call, Visit, See. Never Submit, Click, Buy.

## CTA microcopy examples
- "Chat with us on WhatsApp" (not "Message us")
- "Book your bridal trial" (not "Schedule appointment")
- "See the full gallery" (not "View more")

## Legacy without nostalgia
- "Since 1994" is a power phrase. Use once on Home, once on About. Don't over-use.
- Facts beat sentiment: "30 years", "116 Google reviews", "3 generations of Lahar brides"
- Do not romanticize the past — present-tense the legacy

## Fact claims — permitted vs not
Permitted (verifiable):
- "Since 1994"
- "4.6 stars across 116 Google reviews"
- "Serving brides across Lahar, Bhind, Mihona, Daboh, Gormi, and Datia"
- "One of few salons in the area offering bridal lehenga and jewelry rental"

Not permitted without evidence:
- "Best in India/MP"
- "Award-winning" (unless documented)
- "Celebrity favorite"
- "100% natural"

## FAQ answer format
- 40-80 words per answer
- Start with a direct answer in the first sentence
- Add context in 1-2 sentences
- Close with a next step when useful
